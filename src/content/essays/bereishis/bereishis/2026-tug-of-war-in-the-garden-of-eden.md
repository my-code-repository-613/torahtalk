Life is a daily struggle.  It is a struggle between Good and Evil.  Should I do what is right or should I do what is wrong?

Should I do what I would like to do or should I do what G-d wants me to do?

And it is a lifelong struggle.

Some cultures teach that man is inherently evil; that he was conceived in sin and is compelled to sin.  That is not a Torah concept.

This week’s Torah Portion teaches us that G-d breathed into Adam’s nostrils a living soul that brought him to life.  G-d inserted a bit of Himself into Man, whom He had created in His own image. 

We are inherently G-dly and good, and have the ability to raise ourselves spiritually to celestial heights.

So why is it sometimes so hard to do the right thing?

G-d did not create robots who are instantly programmed to always do G-d’s bidding.  (Actually, that is not totally accurate.  He did create robots.  They are called angels.  We are not angels.)

We have to work to do the right thing.  Man has within himself a Good Inclination and an Evil Inclination.  There is a drive within us which instigates toward doing what is improper and immoral.  The Good Inclination pushes us the other way.

That is the lifelong struggle that G-d wants us to wage.  Without that Evil Inclination we would automatically do what is right.  That would be no big deal.  G-d wants us to fight that Evil inclination and CHOOSE to serve Him.  That is our struggle.

Cain, the oldest son of Adam and Eve, was waging this struggle.  And he was losing.

His brother Abel brought a generous offering with sincerity, which G-d accepted.  Cain, who was not quite as sincere as Abel, brought a cheap offering, which G-d rejected.

Cain was angry.  And he was jealous.   Our Sages tell us that he had other issues with his brother in addition to G-d’s acceptance of his offering over Cain’s.  His Evil Inclination was pushing him to sin further, and he was losing the battle.

*G-d said to him, “Why are you annoyed, and why has your face fallen? Surely if you improve yourself, you will be forgiven…”* (Genesis 4:6-7)

G-d was reminding him that if he tries hard to do what is right he will be rewarded.  But he’s going to have to make sure that his Evil Inclination doesn’t get the better of him…

*“…But if you do not improve yourself, Sin (i.e., the overwhelming urge to sin) rests at the door.  Its desire is toward you, yet you can rule over it.” (ibid).*

There you have it.  The quintessential struggle between right and wrong.  Should Cain improve his attitude, be more sincere in his service of G-d, and be accepting of the differences between his brother and him?

Or should he give into his more base desires?

Cain lost the battle.  He murdered his brother.

To some degree we all go through this struggle every day.  We are constantly faced with decisions to make, and we are always subject to this internal tug-of-war.

And if we lose that war, we have no one to blame but ourselves.

Cain’s sin is the second sin ever committed.  The first was the one committed by his parents.

Adam and Eve were invited into the Garden of Eden; a literal Paradise where all their needs were met.  There was just one catch.  There was this tree…

The snake convinced Eve, and Eve convinced Adam.

G-d asked Adam if he had eaten from the forbidden Tree of Knowledge.  It’s an interesting question.

G-d certainly knew the answer; Adam had given in to temptation.  He had sinned.  The question now was, would he repent?  Would he choose to do the right thing and confess?  Would he choose to do the right thing and ask for forgiveness?

What he did was throw his wife (and G-d!!) under the bus!

*“The wife WHOM YOU GAVE TO BE WITH ME – SHE gave me of the tree, and I ate.”* (ibid, 3:12)

In other words, “It’s not my fault; she (and You!) messed me up!”

What about Eve?  Would she repent?  Would she choose to do the right thing and ask for forgiveness?

What she did was throw the snake under the bus!

*“The snake deceived me, and I ate!”*  (ibid, v.13)

In other words, “It’s not my fault; the snake messed me up.”

Or, in the vernacular, “The Devil made me do it!!”

To be sure, the snake should not have tempted Eve to sin, and Eve should not have tempted Adam to sin.   BUT WHO TOLD THEM TO LISTEN TO BAD ADVICE??!!

At the end of the day we are responsible for our actions.

G-d’s comments to Eve are interesting.

“*Your desire is toward your husband, yet he will rule over you*.” (ibid, v.16)

There are many observations in the various Commentaries as to what exactly G-d was telling Eve.  The following is a suggested additional interpretation of my own.

Eve was created because Adam was alone.  G-d created her to be an advisor and assistant to him.  She was supposed to help Adam serve G-d; instead she undermined him.

“*Your desire is toward your husband, yet he will rule over you*.”

Perhaps G-d was telling her that she was supposed to have been the leader and show him the way.  But now he won’t be so quick to trust her.  Instead, HE will rule over HER!”

And then G-d reprimanded Adam.

“*… you listened to your wife and ate from the tree about which I commanded you saying ‘Don’t eat from it* …’ ” (v.17)

Do you see what G-d appears to be doing here?  He is, in a sense, comparing listening to Eve to obeying the temptations of the Evil Inclination.  Perhaps He is also telling Adam with His words to Eve -- “*… he will rule over you*” – that Adam needs to overrule Eve when she gives him bad advice.

Compare these two verses:

To Cain:  *Its (i.e., Sin’s) desire is toward you, yet you can rule over it.”*

To Eve: “*Your desire is toward your husband, yet he will rule over you*.”

Adam should have, and COULD have ignored his wife’s advice to eat from the tree, and Cain could have, and SHOULD have fought against, and overcome his desire to sin.

Doing the right thing and serving G-d is not always easy.  It’s not supposed to be.  The bigger the challenge, the greater the reward.

If we give in to the temptation to sin, we don’t get to blame the temptation.  If we fail, it is on us.  G-d doesn’t give us a challenge that is too strong for us to overcome.

It is the struggle to overcome that challenge that makes us great. 

\[It is important to make sure not to take the wrong message from the criticism of Eve.  In this particular case she is criticized for not assisting her husband in serving G-d.

The mothers and wives of Israel have distinguished themselves as true spiritual leaders within their families, encouraging their husbands and children and bringing out the best in them.  They truly are those partners and advisors that G-d meant for them to be.

It looks like they learned their lesson from Eve’s mistake!\]
