G-d was busy creating a new world.

There was already a place called Heaven. There was solid ground which He called “Earth”, and a lot of water surrounding it which he called “Seas.”  Now, on the third day of Creation, it was time for some growth:

*G-d said, “Let the earth sprout vegetation:  herbage yielding seed, fruit trees bearing fruit each after its kind…” (Genesis, 1:11)*

Fruit trees bearing fruit?  Isn’t that redundant?  Why didn’t he just order “*fruit trees*” or “*trees bearing frui*t”?

In fact the Torah goes on to appear to ignore this repetition:

*And the earth brought forth vegetation: herbage yielding seed after its kind, and **trees bearing fruit**…  (ibid, v. 12)*

So G-d asked for FRUIT trees bearing fruit, and He got trees bearing fruit.   What happened?  What is the difference between a FRUIT tree bearing fruit and a tree yielding fruit?  Aren’t they both fruit trees?

The Talmud tells us that on the third day of Creation, G-d didn’t get what He asked for. He told the Earth to put forth *FRUIT TREES*, trees that not only produced fruits, but were edible themselves.   A fruit tree, i.e., an edible tree that also produces fruit.  But strangely, the earth disobeyed and produced simply “*trees*”, i.e., wood plants, which yielded fruit!

So the trees were “defective.”  They were supposed to be edible.  Instead of eating apple trees we only get to eat the apple!  Our sages tell us that when Adam and Eve sinned, the earth was cursed as a sinner as well!  

(This concept, the idea of the earth rebelling against G-d and not carrying out His command is a fascinating one.  Are we to assume that the earth had a mind of its own and had the *Chutzah* to disobey its Creator?  Or is this somehow a lesson the Torah wants to teach us about the consequences of disobeying the Creator?  Let’s leave this question for now.  Perhaps we can discuss it another day.)

Last week we celebrated the holiday of Sukkos.  As part of its observance we take four species of vegetation, hold them together, and wave them.  (For more information, see here.  [https://aish.com/what-is-sukkot-customs-blessings-lulav-etrog/](https://aish.com/what-is-sukkot-customs-blessings-lulav-etrog/))

One of these four items is the [esrog](https://www.chabad.org/library/article_cdo/aid/746603/jewish/What-Is-an-Etrog.htm), the citron.  The Talmud (Sukkah 35b) tells us that the esrog is unique in that the fruit tastes like the bark of the tree.   Apparently, the esrog is the one tree that followed G-d’s command!  It is a “*FRUIT tree that bears fruit*!  Could it be that this unique fruit tree, the only one that actually OBEYED G-d’s command was chosen for this Mitzvah to teach us the value of obeying G-d’s Word to the letter?

\----------------------------

When I lived in Arizona, a place with a climate that lends itself to successful citrus tree production, I decided to try my hand at esrog farming.  After Sukkos I cut open my esrog and planted the seeds in cups.  I ended up with four slender saplings, perhaps a foot tall each.  Out of curiosity, I rubbed the leaves between my thumb and index finger and smelled my fingers.  They smelled like esrog!  Could that be part of the fulfilment of G-d’s Commandment back on Day #3?  The leaves of a FRUIT tree?  A tree that is like the fruit?!

For many years I have told people about my amazing discovery of my fruit-like fruit trees. Recently I began to wonder… am I over-inflating this?  Maybe other trees also have this property…

So I asked “Rabbi Google”:  Do other citrus trees have leaves that smell like the fruit?  Sure enough… Busted!!  Leaves that smell like the fruit are a very common trait in most citrus trees.  There went my theory.

Or perhaps not.

[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citron) tells us that the citron “is one of the [original citrus fruits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citrus_taxonomy#Citrons) from which all other citrus types developed through natural [hybrid speciation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_speciation) or artificial [hybridization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_\(biology\)).

When G-d first created the world, He didn’t create all [2500 varieties of citrus fruits that are out there](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_citrus_fruits).   Lemons come from esrog and bitter orange.  Limes come from esrog and papeda. 

So maybe that’s it.  That old Biblical esrog tree followed G-d’s instructions, and it genetically passed along its “edible tree” properties to its decedents!

\----------------------------------------------------

So, now that we have established that so many citrus fruits descend from the esrog, and possess this property of the tree being “fruit-like”, why didn’t I save myself some money last week and use a lemon or a kumquat to celebrate the holiday?

Well, let me tell you a little more about my “esrog orchard” back in Arizona.

When I had my foot-tall saplings growing out of milk containers, I wanted to get some expert guidance in how to get them to grow and flourish, and produce home grown esrogs for me to use in celebrating Sukkos.

There was a member of my congregation who was an agriculture major; I was sure he would have some advice for me.  And he did.

There is a variety of inedible orange called ornamental orange.  It is beautiful, gives off a lovely smell when it blossoms, and is hardy enough to survive the harsh Arizona summers.

He offered to graft my saplings onto ornamental orange roots.  That would give them the toughness to grow in Arizona.

There were two problems with his suggestion.  Grafting different types of fruits together is a [violation of Jewish law](https://outorah.org/p/22338/).  (Poor guy; I probably ruined his major!)

The other problem is that once an esrog is crossbred with another type of fruit, it is no longer considered an esrog!

Citrons are grown commercially for general use in many places throughout the world.  It is very common, and likely, that many, if not most, of these citrons are grafted, and ineligible for use.

So how do you know?

Nowadays people are very careful in verifying the source of their esrog.  There are esrog orchards that have rabbinic supervision ensuring that the product you buy is a genuine esrog.  (See [https://rabbikaganoff.com/the-history-and-halacha-of-grafted-esrogim/](https://rabbikaganoff.com/the-history-and-halacha-of-grafted-esrogim/))

IT LOOKS LIKE AN ESROG, AND SMELLS LIKE AN ESROG, BUT…

I think there is an important lesson we can learn from this.

Christianity has many rituals that stem originally from Judaism.  So does Islam. And while we have no dispute with our gentile neighbors who embrace these lifestyles, they are clearly not Judaism. They are  hybrids.

Over the generations, there have been those who chose to “hybridize” Judaism by borrowing from other faiths.  The whole story of Chanukah is about those who sought to introduce elements of Greek culture into Judaism in order to make it more acceptable to the masses.  They felt that Judaism in its pure form was simply not “palatable.” (Do you have any idea how bitter a pure esrog can be?!!)

G-d gave the Torah to Moses, who passed it down to Joshua and the Elders of Israel, and in every generation it was passed down in an unadulterated manner.  Throughout the ages there were those who sought to hybridize Judaism.  But you can’t improve on perfection.

At the end of the Daily Morning Service, many people have the custom of declaring, based upon the teachings of Maimonides, thirteen Articles of Faith, including, “I believe with complete faith, that the entire Torah now in our hands is the same one given to Moses our teacher, peace be upon him, “ and  “… that this Torah will not be exchanged, nor will there be another Torah from the Creator, Blessed is his Name.”

The Torah that we received on Mount Sinai so many years ago is the same Torah that we just danced with this week on Simchas Torah.

Anything else, as a used car dealer might try to sell you, is a lemon.

Have a great Shabbos.

Rabbi Yerachmiel Seplowitz
